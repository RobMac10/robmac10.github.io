Walking on Ancient Ground

Time Travel on Country · A Choose Your Own Character Adventure

Three lives. One ancient Country. One moment of history, seen through three sets of eyes.

Stand with the Resistor, a warrior defending Country as the newcomers’ farms block the creeks. March beside Thomas Freeman, a First Fleet clerk who goes into the bush with Captain Watkin Tench, hunting a man he begins to suspect is no savage at all. Arrive with Thomas Curtis, a Hampshire labourer who has sailed for one hundred and thirteen days to escape the workhouse, and who meets a Gadigal man named Warra on his first evening lost in the bush.

The book is based on Tench’s journals, colonial records and family memory. It asks: Is it ever right to fight back? What does survival really cost? Can one small kindness change what comes next?

Shipwreckedon Ancient Ground

Shipwrecked on Ancient Ground

Coming soon

In September 1855 the clipper Reindeer strikes a reef in the Coral Sea. Nineteen men reach Port Curtis in a longboat. Weeks later a sheep station nearby is attacked, and the records do not agree about what happened.

Read the accounts of Jo Bennedick, Walter Powell and the Scout, then decide what the records left out.

For readers eleven and older. It includes shipwreck, hunger and frontier violence.

Troubled Pathson Ancient Ground

Troubled Paths on Ancient Ground

Coming soon

The book opens on Anzac Day 1924 at a club in Bundaberg, where a returned soldier named Jack is stopped at the door. It then goes back to the Gahan family’s farm at Kalkie.

On 30 December 1906 the labour agreements of the Pacific Islanders in Queensland end by law. That night two men leave the farm. A farmer, an Islander and a tracker each tell what they saw, and the records do not agree with them.

Resettledon Ancient Ground

Resettled on Ancient Ground

Coming soon

Queensland, 1898. A new law lets the government move Aboriginal people onto reserves. One evening a grandmother and a small girl ask an old Irish woman at Walloon for one night’s shelter. By first light the lean-to is empty.

Three witnesses tell their sides: Mary Browning, Kitty and Archibald Meston, the Southern Protector. Then a case file sets their accounts against the papers.

This book describes the forced control of lives and children under Queensland protection law.
